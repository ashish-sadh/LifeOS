**What happened**: Added all the kale at once (got distracted after garlic step). Pan was too crowded, steam couldn't escape — kale turned gray-green and lost its texture. Flavor was still good but visually unappealing. Lesson confirmed: batches matter.

## 2026-01-12 | Kale pasta — too oily

**What happened**: First attempt using the recipe at full olive oil (5 tbsp as written). Pan was pooling with oil by the end. Dish was edible but greasy and heavy. Coach flagged that home cooks often find restaurant-calibrated olive oil amounts excessive — restaurants use more fat for mouthfeel; for a weeknight at home, less is usually right.

**New vocabulary**: "fat calibration" — adapting recipe fat quantities to your taste and context. Not every recipe is written for the home cook's preference.

**Modification locked in**: 3 tbsp olive oil for this recipe (down from 5). Also logged in recipe file.

**Carry forward**: When trying a new recipe, read the fat quantity with a skeptical eye. Err under — you can always add more.

## 2025-12-01 | Kale pasta — first attempt (dry and clumpy)

**Context**: First time making this dish. No prior experience with pasta-water emulsification.

**What happened**: Pasta clumped immediately after draining. Added it to the pan but everything seized up — no sauce, dry and gluey. Root cause: forgot to reserve pasta water. Tried adding tap water to fix it but the starch is what makes it work.

**New vocabulary**: "pasta water" — starchy, salted water reserved before draining. Essential for finishing sauce-coated pasta. A cup set by the stove acts as a physical reminder.

**Carry forward**: Keep a cup by the stove before starting any pasta dish. Treat it as required equipment.
