# Thai Green Curry

**Source**: Adapted from Pailin's Kitchen (https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/thai-green-curry/) + own testing
**Times tried**: 6
**Last cooked**: 2026-03-22
**Rating**: 4.5/5 (your version)

## Your version (current best)

**Serves**: 3-4  
**Time**: ~40 min (with store-bought paste)

Ingredients:

*Curry base:*
- 1 can (400ml) full-fat coconut milk — do NOT shake before opening
- 3 tbsp Thai green curry paste (Mae Ploy brand, not Mae Anong)
- 1 tbsp fish sauce
- 1 tsp palm sugar (or light brown sugar)
- 4 kaffir lime leaves, torn
- 2 stalks lemongrass, bruised and tied into a knot

*Protein and veg:*
- 500g chicken thighs, cut into 3cm pieces (thigh not breast — stays juicy)
- 1 can baby corn, drained
- 1 medium zucchini, halved and sliced
- Large handful Thai basil (added off heat)

*Serving:*
- Jasmine rice

Method:
1. Open coconut milk without shaking. Spoon thick cream from top into wide pan or wok — about 3-4 tbsp.
2. Heat cream over medium until it starts to bubble and oil separates (watch for shimmer around edges — ~3 min).
3. Add curry paste. Fry in the cream 2 min, stirring constantly. Should smell fragrant, paste darkens slightly.
4. Add chicken, stir to coat in paste. Cook 3-4 min until outside is sealed.
5. Pour in remaining coconut milk (the thin liquid). Add lemongrass, kaffir lime, fish sauce, sugar.
6. Simmer 12-15 min on medium-low until chicken is cooked through and sauce thickens slightly.
7. Add zucchini and corn in last 5 min — they need only brief cooking.
8. Off heat: add Thai basil, stir through. Taste: adjust fish sauce (salty), sugar (sweet), lime juice (sour).
9. Remove lemongrass stalks before serving. Serve over jasmine rice.

## Your modifications from base recipe

- Mae Ploy paste not Mae Anong — Mae Anong is mild and sweet; Mae Ploy has more heat and funk
- Chicken thighs not breast — breast overcooks in the 15-min simmer
- Added zucchini + baby corn (your additions) — original is protein + Thai eggplant; eggplant hard to source
- Palm sugar from Thai grocery — noticeably rounds the flavor versus white sugar
- Thai basil off heat — cooking it kills the fragrance; add at the very end

## What went wrong (and fixes)

- **2026-03-22**: Best attempt yet. Waited for oil separation before adding paste — crucial step you skipped on earlier attempts.
- **2026-02-10**: Sauce too thin and watery. Cause: forgot to skim cream from top of can, added whole can at once. Fix: always scoop cream first.
- **2026-01-05**: Chicken dried out. Cause: used breast, cooked too long. Fix: thighs, and 12 min max once simmering.
- **2025-11-20**: Paste tasted raw/harsh. Cause: didn't fry paste long enough in the cream. Fix: 2 full minutes of frying — you should smell it change.
- **2025-10-18**: Missed the fragrant freshness. Cause: used dried basil. Fix: Thai basil only, fresh, added at end. Supermarket "Thai basil" is often just basil — check.

## Variations to try

- Tofu version: firm tofu pressed and pan-fried before adding — add at end to avoid breaking
- Add eggplant (Thai purple variety) when available — cook same time as zucchini
- Shrimp version: add in last 3 min only, they overcook fast
- Make-your-own paste: once you've nailed the technique with store-bought

## Sourcing notes

- **Mae Ploy paste**: Asian grocery stores (not mainstream supermarkets). Comes in a large tub (~400g) — refrigerate after opening, lasts months.
- **Kaffir lime leaves**: Asian grocery freezer section, or the fresh produce section at larger Asian markets. Freeze extras.
- **Thai basil**: Vietnamese/Thai grocery — looks different from Italian basil (darker, more serrated, purple stems). Worth seeking out.
- **Palm sugar**: same Asian grocery — small discs or tub. Worth it; brown sugar works but slightly different.

## Coach notes

This dish hinges on two techniques: (1) separating coconut cream to fry the paste, and (2) not overcooking the protein. Both are learned by repetition. After 3-4 attempts with these steps explicit, they become second nature. The fish sauce / sugar / lime balance at the end is where personal taste gets expressed — don't skip it.
