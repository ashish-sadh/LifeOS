## 2026-04-22 | Essay: "The Audit" — cutting the backup section

**Context**: Fourth draft, ~3,000 words. Came to session wanting to cut it but not knowing where.

**What happened**: Coach read without commenting first, then: "The strongest paragraph is the spreadsheet one. The weakest is the parents section. Both are doing the same emotional work — but the spreadsheet shows it and the parents section tells it."

**What shifted**: Cut the parents section (650 words). Didn't lose the emotion — it went up. The explicit statement had been making the piece feel over-explained.

**Pattern note**: Every draft of this essay has had a "backup section" — a place where I state the feeling explicitly, as if the showing might not be enough. It's always cuttable. Trusting the showing is still uncomfortable; I'm overriding it with an explicit statement as insurance.

**New vocabulary**: "emotional backup" — the section that names the feeling the piece is already showing. Added to vocabulary.md.

**Carry forward**: In revision: look for any section that explicitly names the feeling. Cut it. The showing is either sufficient or the showing needs work — the backup doesn't fix the showing, it just hides the problem.

## 2026-04-08 | Essay: "The Audit" — structural session

**Context**: Third draft. The middle had expanded — backstory crowding out the present-tense thread.

**What happened**: Coach asked me to summarize the middle section in one sentence. I couldn't. That was the diagnosis — a section you can't summarize doesn't have a single job.

**Exercise done**: Wrote one sentence of purpose for each paragraph in the middle. Found two paragraphs with no clear job (pure backstory, not changing anything). Found three consecutive paragraphs all doing the same job — merged to one.

**What shifted**: Essay went from ~3,600 to ~3,000 words. The middle now has a single job: show the period before the crisis without explaining it.

**Carry forward**: "One sentence per paragraph" test in every revision pass. If I can't write the purpose sentence, the paragraph is not earning its place.

## 2026-03-18 | Voice session — reading old pieces

**Context**: Three months in, coach suggested reading all completed pieces together to look for voice patterns. Had six pieces.

**What happened**: Clear finding: the strongest sentences across all six pieces were short, declarative, and used concrete nouns. The weakest were long compound sentences with multiple subordinate clauses. Compression is where the voice lives.

**Also found**: Three of the six pieces had strong endings but weak openings. Pattern: I warm up into the piece rather than starting in the right moment. The real beginning is usually somewhere in the second or third paragraph.

**Voice finding logged in voice.md**: "Short sentences, concrete nouns, no hedging clauses. Compression is the through-line."

**Carry forward**: Next piece: write the first paragraph last. See if it's stronger.

## 2026-02-25 | Essay: "The Audit" — first draft session

**Context**: First session on the new essay (started from a journal entry about a spreadsheet kept during a period of job searching). Complete first draft, ~2,400 words.

**What happened**: Read together. Coach: "The opening is 400 words of context. The essay starts on page 2." Cut the entire first page. Essay now opens mid-scene at the moment with the spreadsheet.

**New vocabulary**: "earned beginning" vs. "context dump" — an essay can only begin at the moment that earns the reader's interest. Everything before is the writer warming up. Registered in vocabulary.md.

**Profile note**: Recurring pattern confirmed — I write context-setting throat-clearing before finding the actual beginning. The habit runs deep; it's a slow one to break.

**Carry forward**: Next first draft: start from the most specific image or moment you have. Don't lay context first.

## 2026-01-28 | First session — onboarding

**Context**: Came to writing coach after finishing and abandoning a 40,000-word novel draft last year. Goal: smaller pieces, something I can actually complete, figure out whether personal essays are the right form.

**What happened**: Walked through writing history. Established that I generate volume easily but resist revision — I tend to abandon pieces when they resist rather than revise through. Coach framed this as a form problem, not a willpower problem: the novel's feedback loop is too long; essays have tighter ones.

**Profile note**: Generator temperament, not revisor. Need structures that make revision feel like making — not just removing. Essay form might be right.

**Goals agreed**: Complete and submit one personal essay to a literary magazine by end of Q2 2026. "Complete" means at least 5 drafts. Don't start new material — mine existing journal entries.

**Carry forward**: Identify one piece from existing material that could become an essay. Review journal entries from the past year and pick the one that has the most heat.
