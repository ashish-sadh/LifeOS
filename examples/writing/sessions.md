## 2026-09-14 | "The Last Apartment" — structural coaching session

**Context**: Third draft, ~1,240 words. Came with a specific problem: the middle isn't holding together.

**What happened**: Coach read before commenting. Assessment: the premise (gap between anticipated feeling and actual feeling) is right and the ending is found. The problem is the middle — held together by chronology, not by the development of the central feeling.

**Structural insight**: Coach suggested a fourth moment (smaller, quieter) might be better than developing the third moment further. The piece builds by accretion, not by argument. Trust that pattern.

**Voice note**: The "cold/front door" line is the best line in the piece. Coach flagged it as a voice signature — the specific-then-restrained observation. Don't explain it in revision; the explaining would kill it.

**What's working**:
- Parallel structure of "I waited to feel something. I felt X." appearing three times. The rhythm lands.
- The ending. Don't touch it.

**What needs work**:
- Paragraph 4 (previous tenant digression) is the piece trying to be about the apartment again. Cut or justify.
- Middle paragraph 6 runs out of energy before the third moment.

**Profile note**: The first draft of this piece already opened mid-scene without coaching. The context-dump opening habit from "The Audit" first draft appears to be breaking.

**Carry forward**: Cut paragraph 4 first and see if paragraph 6 problem resolves. Read aloud — the piece might be done at ~1,400 words without adding anything.
