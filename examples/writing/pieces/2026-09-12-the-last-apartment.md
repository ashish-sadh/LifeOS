# The Last Apartment

**Form**: Personal essay
**Draft**: 3
**Word count**: 1,240 (target: 1,500–2,000)
**Status**: Revising — structure mostly settled, prose needs one more pass
**Intended audience**: General literary readers
**Submission target**: Consider for Catapult or The Sun (personal essay)

---

## Working premise

About moving out of the apartment where I lived during my thirties — and how leaving it was less sad than I expected, which turned out to be the actually sad thing.

The piece wants to be about the gap between anticipated feeling and actual feeling, but it keeps trying to become a piece about the apartment itself. Need to trust the gap.

---

## Draft excerpt (opening)

The movers arrived at eight, earlier than I'd asked for, and I didn't have coffee yet. This is how I know I was not in the right state for what was about to happen.

My therapist, who I'd been seeing on and off for seven years at that point, had told me that leaving places is hard for me. She meant it as insight. I heard it as instruction. So when the movers started carrying out the couch — the couch I'd had since my first apartment, the one that had hosted a thousand evenings of other people's problems and my own — I waited to feel something.

I felt slightly cold. The front door was open.

The piece wants to end here but I have 1,100 more words to go. Drafts 1 and 2 tried to fill the space with memory. Draft 3 is trying to follow the not-feeling instead.

---

## What changed draft to draft

**Draft 1 → Draft 2**:
- Cut: 800 words of apartment-specific memory (the bedroom window, the neighbor who played piano, the summer the AC broke). All of it was good and none of it was the point.
- Added: the therapist frame in the opening. This is what the piece is actually about — the gap between prescribed feeling and real feeling.
- Still broken: the middle. It's a series of observations held together by chronology, not argument.

**Draft 2 → Draft 3**:
- Restructured middle around three moments where I expected to feel something and didn't. Parallel structure is working better.
- Found the ending: ends on something small and unresolved — I kept a single coat hook. It felt right immediately.
- Still needs work: paragraph 4 is still doing the apartment-memory thing. Cut or justify.

## Revision notes (current)

Open problems:
- **Paragraph 4**: The digression about the previous tenant is interesting but it's the piece trying to be about the apartment again. Cut unless it earns its place — currently it doesn't.
- **Word count gap**: At 1,240 I need 260–760 more words. Resist the urge to fill — the piece might be done at ~1,400. Test by reading aloud.
- **Opening is strong, close is strong, middle paragraph 6 is weak**: sequence runs out of energy. The third moment (the coat hook decision) needs more weight before the ending.

What's working:
- The cold/front door line. Best line in the piece.
- The parallel structure of "I waited to feel something. I felt X." — appearing three times. The rhythm is landing.
- The ending. Don't touch it.

## Feedback received

**Workshop reader (Sep 10)**:
- "The opening is disarming — the wrongfooting works."
- "I lost you in the middle. By paragraph 5 I wasn't sure where we were going."

## Coach notes

*Added after session 2026-09-14*:

The premise is right and the ending is found. This is unusual — most pieces don't find their ending until much later. The risk now is that revision becomes polishing before the middle is load-bearing.

The voice is working: the cold/front door line is exactly the kind of specific-then-restrained observation that's a signature in the best essays. Don't over-explain it in revision.

One structural suggestion: the middle might be better served by a fourth moment (smaller, quieter) rather than developing the third moment further. The piece is building by accretion, not argument — trust that pattern.

Cross-reference: [[voice#Signatures]] — paragraph endings that stop one beat early. The close does this. The middle paragraphs don't yet.
