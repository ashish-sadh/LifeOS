## 2026-09-15 | Decision: VP of Product at Meridian — accepted

**Context**: Worked through this decision over two sessions (Sep 8 and Sep 15). Most consequential decision logged with coach to date.

**What happened**: Applied inversion early — "what would have to be true for this to be the wrong call?" - "Meridian raises a Series B by end of 2027." Confidence: 55%.

**Process note**: The written process made the unknowns legible in a way that in-head analysis never does. Contrast with November 2025 job mistake — same type of decision, but run entirely in my head. The difference in process quality is visible.

**Open gap**: "Why did the previous VP Product leave?" — got a non-answer in the final round. Accepted anyway. Flag to resolve in the first month.

**Carry forward**: Log full write-up in decisions/. Set 6-month outcome review for 2027-03-15.

## 2026-05-12 | Second-order thinking applied — hiring decision

**Context**: Team was considering adding a PM mid-Q2 to address capacity. Asked coach to help think through it before responding to the staffing request.

**What happened**: First-order: more capacity = better. Second-order analysis: hiring mid-cycle adds onboarding overhead during the period you most need output; signals to stakeholders that scope isn't under control; resets team trust dynamics. The second-order costs outweighed the first-order benefit.

**Decision**: Don't hire mid-Q2. Address scope instead — cut two low-leverage workstreams.

**Model applied**: Second-order thinking — first time used explicitly rather than implicitly. The model was in vocabulary.md as a concept but this is the first logged application.

**Pattern note**: The instinct was to say yes immediately (capacity = good). The second-order analysis took 20 minutes and reversed the call. The instinct was first-order only.

**Vocabulary updated**: added concrete example to second-order thinking entry in vocabulary.md.

**Carry forward**: Before any resource request response, ask "what happens in the quarter after we add this?" One step further than the immediate consequence, every time.
