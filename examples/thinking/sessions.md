**Carry forward**: Before assigning any confidence level to an interpersonal prediction, find one historical comparable and use it as an anchor.

## 2026-04-14 | Decision: skip the conference or present

**Context**: Invited to submit a talk proposal to a regional product conference. Noticed the pull to say yes immediately — it felt validating. Checked in with coach before responding.

**Model applied — inversion**: "What would have to be true for this to be the wrong call?" Answer: I'd need to not have a point of view worth 45 min of an audience's time, or the timing would be wrong. Both are true right now — Q2 is peak-load and I don't have a developed talk concept.

**Model applied — pre-mortem**: Imagined giving the talk and it going badly. Plausible scenario: vague thesis, insufficient rehearsal, 3 weekends spent on slides during the busiest quarter. The scenario wasn't far-fetched.

**Decision**: Pass this round; submit to the fall edition.

**Coaching note**: The initial pull toward yes was validation-seeking, not content-driven enthusiasm. Worth flagging as a bias pattern — I commit to visibility opportunities before checking whether I have something worth saying.

**Profile note**: New failure mode logged — "visibility pull." Logged in patterns.md.

**Carry forward**: When invited to a speaking or high-visibility opportunity: before responding, ask "do I have the content to justify this right now?" If the honest answer is no, defer.

## 2026-03-15 | Stuck on a team conflict

**Context**: Had a difficult conversation with a senior IC on the team — they felt their input wasn't being incorporated. Came to session feeling reactive and defensive.

**What happened**: Coach asked: "What would you believe about this situation if you were them?" That question broke the defensiveness enough to engage. Wrote out the opposing case — what the IC might reasonably believe about how decisions were being made.

**Insight**: I had been treating the disagreement as a communication style problem when it was actually a process transparency problem. The IC wasn't asking for more input — they were asking to see the criteria by which input gets weighed.

**Model applied**: Steelmanning (accidentally). Writing out the opposing case produced a more accurate diagnosis than I had reached through direct reflection.

**Profile note**: Under conflict I skip to "how do I handle this person?" instead of "what are they actually claiming?" The slower move — accurately model their claim — produces better outcomes.

**Carry forward**: When feeling defensive in a disagreement, use "what would I believe if I were them?" before formulating any response.

## 2026-01-20 | First session — onboarding

**Context**: Started working with thinking coach after a significant decision made last November (accepted a role that turned out to be a poor fit). Goal: build a more reliable decision process.

**What happened**: Walked through the November decision in detail. Root-cause analysis: I had gathered opinions only from people who were already enthusiastic about the opportunity — confirmation bias in who I consulted. I also hadn't written down what I was optimizing for; the analysis ran entirely in my head and shifted depending on my mood.

**Models introduced**:
- Decision journaling (pre-decision write-up + outcome log)
- Confirmation bias — I knew the name but hadn't recognized my specific version

**Profile note**: Primary failure mode confirmed: I consult selectively when decisions are emotionally charged. Need an explicit "seek the opposing view" step in my process.

**Goals set**: Use a decision pre-mortem for every significant decision this quarter. Log predictions with confidence percentages.

**Carry forward**: Decision journaling template created in decisions/. First real entry: conference proposal coming in Q2.
