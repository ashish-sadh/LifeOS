# 2026-09-15 — Accept the VP of Product offer at Meridian?

**Status**: decided — accepted
**Decision deadline**: 2026-09-22
**Tags**: #career #high-stakes #compensation

## Context

Current role: Senior PM at a 300-person Series B. Comfortable, good team, limited upside.

The offer: VP of Product at Meridian (80-person Series A, 18 months of runway). Step up in seniority and scope. Significant equity. Lower base (~15% cut). Joining a team I mostly respect but haven't worked with.

Runway concern: 18 months is fine if growth trajectory holds — but if they miss Q4, they raise in a bad position.

## Considerations

For:
- Title and scope jump I wouldn't get at current company for 3+ years
- Domain (climate infrastructure) is where I want to be long-term
- Equity could be meaningful if they exit — rough numbers are reasonable
- CEO has shipped before (not first-time founder)

Against:
- 18 months of runway — risk is real, not hypothetical
- Lower base is a real cost, especially given current savings goal
- I don't know the engineering lead well; our working styles might clash
- Partner is in a career transition too — two simultaneous bets is more exposure

Unknowns I couldn't resolve:
- Q3 revenue number (they shared Q2 only)
- Why previous VP Product left (got non-answer)

## Mental models I used

- **[[models/inversion]]** — "How does this go badly?" → runway cliff, engineering-PM friction, title gain without real authority. Helped me decide to ask hard questions in the final round.
- **[[models/regret-minimization]]** — Which choice would I regret more at 45? Staying was the safer answer in the short term but the one I'd resent.
- **[[models/opportunity-cost]]** — The direct comparison isn't Meridian vs. current job. It's Meridian vs. the *next* opportunity that comes along. How often does a VP role in climate infrastructure appear? Roughly once every 2 years at my network depth.

## My prediction (before deciding)

If I accept: I'll be glad I did by end of 2027, regardless of company outcome.
Confidence: 72%

If Meridian raises a Series B by end of 2027: 55%

If I stay: I'll be interviewing again within 18 months.
Confidence: 80%

## Decision

Accepted. Negotiated a 6-month cliff waiver on 25% of equity given the runway situation. They accepted.

Start date: 2026-10-15.

## Reflection — written 1 week after deciding

The decision process was better than usual. Using inversion early forced me to get specific about failure modes instead of just feeling anxious. The regret-minimization frame helped most — it moved me out of present-tense optimization into longer-arc thinking.

What I'd do differently: resolve the "why did VP Product leave" question before the final round, not after. I accepted without a clean answer. That's a data gap I should have pushed harder on.
