# Pole Vocabulary

Each entry: what it is mechanically, the muscles, prerequisites, common failure modes, the user's status with it.

---

## Chopper

**What**: Foundational invert from the floor. Both legs lift toward the ceiling, body forms a V or pike against the pole. Pole gripped two-handed; feet/legs do not touch the pole during entry.

**Muscles**: Hip flexors (raise legs), abs (lower abs + obliques), lats and biceps (pull body to pole), grip strength.

**Prerequisites**: Strong-enough core to lift legs against gravity; grip strength to hold body weight; comfort inverting.

**Common failure modes**:
- Swinging into it (uses momentum, builds bad habits)
- Bent arms (load shifts to biceps)
- Disengaged scaps (round shoulders → trap pain)

**User's status**: <new / working / clean>

---

## Crucifix

**What**: Inverted hold; one leg hooked at knee/thigh on pole; arms release; body extends horizontally in cross shape.

**Muscles**: Hooked-leg adductors, obliques, low back, core stabilizers.

**Prerequisites**: Confident leg hang; core strength to hold horizontal without arms.

**Common failure modes**:
- Hips dropping below horizontal
- Loose leg hook → slipping
- Holding breath (limits duration)

**User's status**: <new / working / clean>

---

## Inside leg hang

**What**: One-leg hold using inner thigh as the hook against pole. Body inverts; inside of thigh presses pole.

**Muscles**: Inner thigh adductors, core, lats (during entry).

**Prerequisites**: Comfort inverting from chopper; pinch strength in inner thigh.

**Common failure modes**:
- Hooking too low on thigh (less stable)
- Not actively pinching (relying on hook alone)

**User's status**: <new / working / clean>

---

## Outside leg hang

**What**: One-leg hold using outer thigh / back of knee on opposite side of pole. Body inverts on the opposite side from inside leg hang.

**Muscles**: Outer thigh / hamstrings, core, obliques (twist body away from pole).

**Prerequisites**: Inside leg hang; rotation skill to flip from inside to outside.

**Common failure modes**:
- Insufficient hip rotation away from pole
- Slipping out of the hook

**User's status**: <new / working / clean>

---

## Butterfly

**What**: Inverted shape; cup grip (both hands stacked, palms forward); body opens away from pole, one leg extended.

**Muscles**: Lats, triceps (push body away in cup grip), obliques, glute (extended leg).

**Prerequisites**: Cup grip strength; chopper invert; hip-opening into shape.

**Common failure modes**:
- Bent extended leg (shape collapses)
- Insufficient hip rotation (looks like tuck, not open butterfly)
- Cup grip slipping (sweaty hands, weak triceps)

**User's status**: <new / working / clean>

---

## Climbing

**What**: Ascending the pole; one foot front, one back, hooked; arms reach alternating.

**Muscles**: Grip, lats, biceps, hip flexors, inner thigh, core.

**Prerequisites**: Trust in leg hook; grip endurance.

**Common failure modes**:
- Climbing fast and shallow (bad foot placement)
- Over-reliance on arms (grip burnout)

**User's status**: <new / working / clean>

---

## Shoulder mount

**What**: Inversion entry; grip pole, place shoulder against pole, drive hips overhead. Very strength-dependent.

**Muscles**: Core (especially lower abs), hip flexors, scaps + lats, grip.

**Prerequisites**: Strong core; scapular awareness (CRITICAL — improper scap engagement causes trap injuries here); shoulder mobility.

**Common failure modes**:
- Round shoulders / disengaged scaps → trap injury (#1 risk)
- Insufficient hip drive
- Wrong shoulder placement
- Trying to muscle without core engagement

**User's status**: <new / working / clean>

**Coaching emphasis**: Trap-protective scap engagement is non-negotiable.
