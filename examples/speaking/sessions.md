## 2026-04-30 | Post-mortem — PlatformX internal talk

**Context**: Gave a 25-min internal talk on system migration learnings to ~80 engineers and managers. First internal talk at this scale.

**What landed**: The narrative framing worked — "what we expected to be hard vs. what was actually hard" subverted the audience's assumption and made the first 3 minutes feel alive. Q&A ran 12 minutes over the allotted 5. That's the best signal a technical talk can produce.

**What didn't**: The final 5 minutes got compressed. Ran long in the middle section and the ending felt thin. Two separate people mentioned "felt like it just stopped." They were right.

**Pattern note**: Third talk with a weak ending. The middle always gets the prep time; the close gets what's left over. This is backwards — the close is what they carry out of the room.

**Carry forward**: For the next talk: draft the closing paragraph before anything else. If I can't close it, I don't know what the talk is for.

## 2026-04-15 | Pre-talk prep — PlatformX migration talk

**Context**: One week before the internal talk. Working through structure and opening.

**What happened**: First draft had three different opening options. Coach asked: "Which of these is about the audience, not about you?" That cut it immediately to one. Landed on: "How many of you have estimated a migration and been right?" — pause — "Right, me neither." Got a laugh in practice and set up the whole talk.

**New vocabulary**: "audience-facing opening" — an opening that names something the audience already feels or believes, rather than establishing your own context or credentials. Registered in vocabulary.md.

**Structure finalized**: Problem setup (2 min) → what we expected (3 min) → what actually happened (12 min) → 3 transferable lessons (5 min) → close (3 min). Word-count estimated per section.

**Carry forward**: The close isn't tight yet — only drafted in bullet points. Rehearse it as full sentences before the talk.

## 2026-03-20 | Skill session — filler word drill

**Context**: Coach flagged in the previous session that "so" is used as a re-entry word after pauses — appeared 14 times in a recorded internal meeting review. Working to replace "so" with silence.

**What happened**: Did 3 × 2-min extemporaneous recordings.

| Recording | Total fillers | "so" | other |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 | 7 | 1 "um" |
| 2 | 5 | 4 | 1 "like" |
| 3 | 3 | 2 | 1 "um" |

**Pattern confirmed**: Fillers cluster at sentence openings after a pause — reaching for a connector word instead of just starting the next point. Not mid-sentence.

**Insight**: Replacing "so" with a deliberate pause is actually stronger, not just filler-free. The pause lets the last sentence land before the next one starts.

**Carry forward**: In prepared talk rehearsal, mark every sentence-opening "so" in the script. Replace with a pause. Track count per rehearsal.

## 2026-02-10 | First session — onboarding

**Context**: Started working with speaking coach after getting feedback that delivery felt "tentative" in a demo to exec leadership. Goal: give a conference talk in 2026 and build delivery confidence generally.

**Self-assessment from intake**: Comfortable in groups under 10; anxiety ramps up from ~20 upward. Specific form: first 60-90 seconds are physically rough (voice tightens, elevated heart rate), then settles if the opening is solid.

**Profile note**: "Opening-dependent anxiety" — once the first 90 seconds are past, delivery usually stabilizes. Implication: over-rehearse the opening. A cold-rehearsed opening that goes smoothly can break the anxiety cycle; a stumbled opening sustains it.

**Goals agreed**:
1. Filler word count under 5 per 10-min recording
2. Build and deliver a 25-min internal talk by Q2
3. Submit to one external conference by Q3

**Carry forward**: Record one 2-min extemporaneous take per week. Track filler count.
