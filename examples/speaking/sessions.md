## 2026-09-15 | Post-mortem — Q4 Engineering Strategy (all-hands)

**Context**: Gave a 20-min talk + 10 min Q&A on Q4 engineering strategy to ~60 engineers and managers. Full prep (three rehearsals Sep 12-14).

**What landed**:
- Opening hit exactly as intended. "In Q3 we shipped more than any quarter in company history. And almost none of it hit the metric we actually care about." Room went quiet. Multiple people mentioned it afterward.
- Three-project structure was clear — engineering leads knew which bucket their work was in.
- Q&A substantive and friendly. The skeptical lead I'd been worried about asked a fair question and engaged with the answer.

**What didn't**:
- Project 2's "why now" is still muddier than 1 and 3. One IC said afterward they still don't know what "done" looks like for project 3 — valid; I spent 90 seconds on it instead of the planned 2.5 min.
- Looked at slides too much during the three-project section. Under-memorized the project specifics.
- Close was better than in rehearsal but still slightly rushed.

**Pattern observed**: [[themes/rushing-the-close]] — appeared again. Third talk in a row. Now explicitly flagged as active work.

**Carry forward**:
1. Next talk: draft the closing paragraph first, before anything else. It's the only thing the audience carries out.
2. For any multi-project talk: rehearse each "done" definition separately until it's 3 sentences, no slide reference needed.

## 2026-08-20 | Pre-talk framing — Q4 Engineering Strategy

**Context**: Invited to present Q4 engineering strategy at all-hands. Started framing 3 weeks out.

**What happened**: First instinct was to present the three projects and explain the rationale for each. Coach asked: "What do you want people to *do differently* after this talk?" That reframed everything. The answer: flag blockers early instead of routing around them. That became the close.

**Structure settled**: Q3 observation → three projects (not seven) → "done" definition per project → one ask for the room.

**Opening line**: Start with the Q3 observation — a statement, not a question. Let it land before moving on. "In Q3 we shipped more than any quarter in the company's history. And almost none of it hit the metric we actually care about."

**Carry forward**: Rehearse the opening cold before writing the rest. Get the first sentence automatic before building out.

## 2026-04-30 | Post-mortem — PlatformX internal talk

**Context**: Gave a 25-min internal talk on system migration learnings to ~80 engineers and managers. First internal talk at this scale.

**What landed**: The narrative framing worked — "what we expected to be hard vs. what was actually hard" subverted the audience's assumption and made the first 3 minutes feel alive. Q&A ran 12 minutes over the allotted 5.

**What didn't**: The final 5 minutes got compressed. Ran long in the middle section and the ending felt thin. Two separate people mentioned "felt like it just stopped."

**Pattern note**: Second talk with a weak ending. The middle always gets the prep time; the close gets what's left.

**Carry forward**: For next talk: draft the closing paragraph before anything else. If I can't close it, I don't know what the talk is for.

## 2026-03-20 | Skill session — filler word drill

**Context**: "so" appeared 14 times in a recorded meeting review. Working to replace it with silence.

**What happened**: Three 2-min extemporaneous recordings.

| Recording | Fillers | "so" | other |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 | 7 | 1 "um" |
| 2 | 5 | 4 | 1 "like" |
| 3 | 3 | 2 | 1 "um" |

**Pattern confirmed**: Fillers cluster at sentence openings after a pause — reaching for a connector instead of just starting the next point.

**Insight**: Replacing "so" with a deliberate pause is stronger, not just cleaner. The pause lets the last sentence land.

**Carry forward**: Mark every sentence-opening "so" in prepared talk scripts. Replace with a pause.

## 2026-02-10 | First session — onboarding

**Context**: Feedback after an exec demo: delivery felt "tentative." Goal: give a conference talk in 2026 and build delivery confidence.

**Self-assessment**: Comfortable under 10 people; anxiety ramps from ~20 upward. First 60-90 seconds are rough (voice tightens), then usually settles if the opening is solid.

**Profile note**: "Opening-dependent anxiety" — over-rehearse the opening. A cold-rehearsed opening that goes smoothly can break the anxiety cycle.

**Goals agreed**:
1. Filler word count under 5 per 10-min recording
2. Build and deliver a 25-min internal talk by Q2
3. Submit to one external conference by Q3

**Carry forward**: Record one 2-min extemporaneous take per week. Track filler count.
