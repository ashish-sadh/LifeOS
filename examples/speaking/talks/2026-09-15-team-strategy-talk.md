# 2026-09-15 — Q4 Engineering Strategy

**Venue**: All-hands engineering meeting (~60 people, internal)
**Format**: 20-minute talk + 10 min Q&A
**Status**: delivered
**Outcome**: landed well — strong Q&A engagement, several follow-up conversations

---

## Talk brief

**The one thing**: The Q4 strategy is a deliberate bet on depth over breadth — fewer projects, finished faster, shipped to users. Not a resource conversation.

**Audience**: Engineering org (ICs, leads, PMs listening in). Mix of people who've heard strategy context and people who haven't. A few leads who were skeptical of the prioritization decisions.

**What I need them to leave with**:
- Understand the *why* behind the scope cut (not just that it happened)
- Trust that the three remaining projects are the right ones
- Know what "done" looks like for each by December

**What I don't need them to do**: agree on methodology — that's a separate conversation. Just accept the frame.

---

## Structure

| Segment | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Opening — the observation | 2 min | Start with what we all saw in Q3: shipped a lot, landed little. Name it. |
| The choice we made | 3 min | Three projects vs. seven. Explain the filter: user value × feasibility × now vs. later. |
| The three projects (what + why each) | 8 min | 2.5 min each. Don't cover specs — cover why *this* is the right bet. |
| What "done" means by December | 4 min | Specific. Ship X to Y users. Not "complete the build." |
| Close — what I need from this room | 2 min | One ask: flag blockers early, don't route around them. |
| Buffer | 1 min | |

**Opening line (rehearsed cold)**:
"In Q3 we shipped more than any quarter in the company's history. And almost none of it hit the metric we actually care about. That's the thing I want to talk about today."

## Rehearsal notes

- Run 1 (Sep 12): 23 min — too long. Cut the "methodology" section entirely. Nobody needs to understand the scoring model.
- Run 2 (Sep 13): 19 min — still too slow on project 2. Memorize the 3-sentence version of each project.
- Run 3 (Sep 14, with one colleague): 20 min, good. Feedback: "The opening is strong. The close felt rushed — slow down there, it's the ask."

**Delivery notes to self**:
- Pause after the opening line. Let it land.
- For each project: one sentence on what, one on why now, one on what done looks like. No more.
- Slow down the close — that's the only thing I'm actually asking for. Don't rush it.
- If a skeptical lead challenges the prioritization in Q&A, validate the concern before answering: "That's the call I was least confident in — here's the reasoning."

## Post-event debrief

**Date**: 2026-09-15 (same day)

**What worked**:
- Opening landed exactly as intended. Room got quiet. Several people told me afterward the Q3 framing was exactly what they needed to hear.
- The three-project structure was clear — engineering leads knew which bucket their work was in.
- Q&A was substantive and friendly. The skeptical lead (the one I was worried about) asked a fair question and seemed satisfied with the answer.

**What I'd change**:
- Project 2 explanation is still muddier than 1 and 3. I don't have a crisp "why now" for it. Need to work on that before I give any version of this talk again.
- I looked at slides too much in the middle section. Probably because I hadn't memorized the project details as well as the framing.
- The close was better than in rehearsal but still felt slightly rushed.

**Feedback received**:
- "Strong opening — you named the thing nobody wanted to name." (Engineering lead)
- "Clearer than I expected given how much we cut. I understand the bets now." (PM lead, in follow-up Slack)
- "Could've gone deeper on project 3 — I still don't know what done looks like for that one." (IC, in 1:1 after) → valid, I spent 90 seconds on it instead of 2.5

**Themes surfaced** (cross-link):
- [[themes/opening-with-a-question]] — actually opened with an observation, not a question. More effective.
- [[themes/rushing-the-close]] — showed up again. Third talk in a row.

**Next version of this talk** (if I give it again):
- Sharpen project 2's "why now" framing
- Expand project 3's "done" definition to 3 sentences
- Practice the close last in every rehearsal, not first
