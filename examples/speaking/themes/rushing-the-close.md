# Rushing the close

**Pattern type**: delivery — pacing
**First observed**: 2026-04-30
**Times observed**: 3
**Trend**: recurring — not improving without active attention

## What it is

The closing segment of a talk — the ask, the call to action, the "what I need from this room" — is delivered faster than any other segment. Even when the close has been rehearsed, it speeds up under the pressure of wrapping up.

## Why it matters

The close is the only part of a talk where the speaker is making an explicit ask. Rushing it undermines:
- Audience recall of what the speaker wants (they're already mentally leaving)
- Speaker authority — confidence communicates slowness, not speed
- Q&A setup — a rushed close produces vague opening questions

## Where it shows up

- Internal presentations with time pressure
- Any talk longer than 15 minutes (fatigue factor)
- Talks where the middle section ran long and the speaker is compensating

## Root cause (current hypothesis)

The close is always last in rehearsal and therefore gets less practice time per run. Also: when the middle runs long, the natural instinct is to compress the close to stay on time — but the close is the worst place to compress.

## Fix protocol

1. **Rehearsal order**: practice the close first in at least one full run, not last
2. **Explicit time mark**: note the clock time when the close should start (e.g., "start close at T+17:00 of a 20-min talk") and hit it regardless of middle section length — cut the middle if needed
3. **Verbal cue to self**: before the close segment, take one breath and reset pace
4. **Deliberate pause**: after the transition to close ("So — the one thing I'm asking for..."), pause for 2 seconds before continuing
